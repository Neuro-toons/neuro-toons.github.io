Cold, scared or moved by a song? Your skin goes bumpy. That's your **sympathetic nervous system** — the fight-or-flight system — at work.

## What's happening

1. Your brain triggers a sympathetic response.
2. Sympathetic nerves release **noradrenaline** onto tiny muscles at the base of each hair: the **arrector pili**.
3. Those muscles contract, pulling the hair upright and bunching the skin around it into a bump.

## Why bother?

In furry animals, raised hair traps a layer of warm air and makes the animal look bigger to a threat. In humans, with so little body hair, it's mostly a leftover — but the reflex is still wired in.

> **Exam tip:** goosebumps = sympathetic activation → arrector pili contraction.
