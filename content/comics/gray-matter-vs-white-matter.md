Slice a brain and you'll see two shades: **gray matter** and **white matter**. The difference isn't decoration — it tells you what each region *does*.

## Gray matter: the thinkers

Gray matter is packed with **neuron cell bodies, dendrites and synapses**. This is where information is received, combined and processed.

## White matter: the wiring

White matter is mostly **myelinated axons** — the long cables that carry signals between regions. Myelin is rich in lipids, which gives it its pale, white colour and makes signals travel much faster.

## Where is each one?

- **Brain:** gray matter on the *outside* (the cortex), white matter inside.
- **Spinal cord:** flipped — gray matter in the butterfly-shaped core, white matter around it.

> **Exam tip:** Gray = processing (cell bodies). White = communication (myelinated axons).
